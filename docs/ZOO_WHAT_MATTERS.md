# Build A Zoo: What Matters On The Screen

Written 5 September 2026. Companion to frames 13, 13b and 14 in this folder. It follows the live-app review of the same day and answers one question: why the screen is still confusing after the layout fixes, and what to do about it.

## The Problem

The layout is now close to the mock. The tabs, the bench, the board, the strip and the dock are all in. The screen is still confusing, and the reason is not where things are. It is that everything has the same weight.

A learner in the middle of a build day sees a strip of twelve equal pills, three tabs, a heading, chips, a toggle, a goal band, a rail, a bench, a park and a dock. Each is the same size and the same colour as its neighbour. The clock, which is the one element that should change what the learner does next, is one of the twelve pills. Four of the others are abbreviations with no values and no explanation.

When nothing is bigger, nothing is important. That is the confusion.

## What Matters, In Order

During a build day the learner needs three things.

1. **How much of today is left.** The clock. It decides whether to start something or finish something.
2. **Is the Sprint Goal safe.** Essentials done and points done, in one line, beside the clock. It decides what to start.
3. **What is in my hand.** The item and its next step. It decides what to do right now.

Everything else is reference: the goal text, the value measures, the Scrum definitions, the artifacts, the notes. Reference is read once and looked up later. It belongs in one place the learner opens, not in the strip.

## The Screen Under The Strip

In Build state the attention order is drawn, not described.

- The board rail is greyed to 60%. It is there if you want it; it does not ask.
- The item in hand is the only white card. Its title is the largest text on the tab.
- The next step is the only orange box.
- The park's stats are one line on the park: "Increment 0 · Sites 1 · 480 visitors".
- The controls are a single grey line at the foot of the card until a part of the site is touched.

Counted: clock, goal line, next step, park. Four things asking for attention.

## The Learn Drawer

Frame 14. One drawer, opened from the one button, with four sections.

**Scrum.** The Scrum Reference drawer as it is today: team, events, artifacts, one line each, open for more. It was already the right shape. It becomes the model for the rest.

**Value.** The four key value measures, each as a card with the name, today's number, what it is in one line, how the zoo computes it, and what moves it. For example: Current Value, 62, how happy today's visitors are; average happiness across the three groups out of 100; rises when Done work opens, falls when a group finds nothing for them. This is the metrics explanation the live app lacks. During the Sprint the numbers live here. At the Review they return to the screen, where they have values and a sentence each.

**This Sprint.** The Sprint Goal, the bet, the Definition of Done and the decision log so far. This is the Artifacts drawer, folded in.

**Notes.** The rail messages, kept for the Sprint, and every "What is this?" text from every panel. The paragraphs that currently open each card move here, and the cards keep their titles.

What goes: the Artifacts button, the Scrum button, the help icons, the explanatory paragraphs on the Product Backlog card, the Increment tab, the bench placeholder, the split dialogue and the sizing panel. Each is one entry in Learn.

## The Rule Behind It

The screen keeps the work. The drawer keeps the words.

Anything the learner acts on stays on the screen at a weight that matches how often they act on it. Anything the learner reads to understand goes in Learn, grouped so it can be found. The clock is acted on every minute, so it is the biggest thing. A definition of Time to Market is read once, so it is a card in a drawer.

## One Decision Left

Whether the value dials leave the strip during the Sprint or stay in small grey type under the clock. Leaving is simpler and the frames assume it. Staying keeps the four letters in view so they are familiar by the Review. Either is defensible. What is not defensible is the dials at the same weight as the clock, which is what the live app does today.

## Order To Build

1. The strip: clock large with bar and colour change, goal line, Learn button. Remove the rest.
2. The Learn drawer with the Scrum Reference moved in and the Value section written.
3. Fold the Artifacts drawer into This Sprint; the rail and the help texts into Notes.
4. Build state attention order: rail greyed, item in hand as the one white card, next step as the one orange box.
5. The warning state: bar colour, goal line text, the one sentence.

Steps one and two do most of the work. The rest is tidying.
