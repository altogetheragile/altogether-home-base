# Giving The Scrum Master A Job

Written 6 September 2026. Drawing on Barry Overeem's *The 8 Stances of a Scrum Master* (Scrum.org whitepaper, v2.0, May 2017) to close the gap named in `ZOO_ACCOUNTABILITIES_PROPOSAL.md`: of the three accountabilities, the Scrum Master is the one with nothing to do.

The paper is Scrum.org's copyright. We build on its ideas and cite it; its text and its drawings do not go in the product. The stance names are common vocabulary, the framing here is ours.

## The Gap, Restated

The game teaches artifacts by making you hold them and events by making you attend them. It teaches accountabilities by naming them. The Product Owner orders the Backlog and writes the Goal; the Developers estimate, plan and build; the Scrum Master watches.

The raw material is already there and wasted. Impediments arrive at the Daily Scrum, cost time, and offer two answers - "adapt the plan" or "carry on regardless" - neither of which is a Scrum Master act. The impediment is weather, not a problem anybody solves.

## What The Paper Gives Us

Eight preferred stances (servant leader, coach, facilitator, teacher, mentor, manager, impediment remover, change agent) and eight misunderstood ones (scribe, secretary, Scrum police, team boss, admin, chairman, super hero, coffee clerk).

**Three of the eight mechanise. Five are teaching content.** Saying so plainly is the point of this note: a stance forced into a button becomes a quiz with a right answer, which is the thing the deck's discussion exercises must never become.

### 1. Impediment Remover - the one with a real decision in it

The paper's most game-able idea is a distinction we do not currently make: **a block affects one task and the Developers can clear it themselves; an impediment slows the whole team's progress and needs the Scrum Master.** Today the game calls everything an impediment and gives you a button, so the answer is always the same and nothing is learned.

It also gives the test to put on the screen - is it really an impediment, or something the team can resolve themselves? do we actually need to remove it? what is the real problem here? - and the arbiter: **use the Sprint Goal.** If it prevents the Sprint Goal, it is an impediment. The game already computes exactly that (`goalPulse`), so it can hold that line honestly rather than by assertion.

What this becomes:

| Arrives as | Left alone | Removed by the Scrum Master | Worked around |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Block** (one item) | the Developers clear it in their own time, at a small cost to that item | costs the Scrum Master's time AND teaches the team to wait | the item takes longer |
| **Impediment** (the team) | it grows overnight - what happens today | cleared, at a real cost in time now | a smaller cut today, and it returns worse |

Neither table cell is forbidden. Removing a block is the Super Hero; leaving an impediment is the absent Scrum Master. The game does not score either, it names the pattern once it is a habit.

### 2. Manager - the frame for the Scrum Master's screen

The paper's manager stance lists five things a Scrum Master manages: impediments and waste, the process, the team's health, the boundaries of self-management, and the culture. Four of the five have something in this game to attach to:

- **impediments and waste** - the board below;
- **the process** - whether the events happen and stay in their timebox (the Daily Scrum is skippable, and the game charges for it);
- **the team's health** - a mirror at the Review, not a score: goal protected when scope had to give, impediments removed against endured, Daily Scrums held;
- **the boundaries of self-management** - the WIP limit is the Developers' agreement, and the Scrum Master's job is the conversation about it, not the number.

Culture sits outside one zoo. It belongs to the between-Sprints organisation beat, or to the trainer.

### 3. Facilitator - already half built

"Setting the stage and providing clear boundaries." In this game that is the timebox: the Daily Scrum's countdown, the events happening at all, and what it costs when they do not. It needs no new mechanic, only attribution - the Scrum Master is accountable for the event happening, not for running it.

## The Misunderstood Stances Are The Gameplay

A stance is hard to perform in a game. An anti-pattern is easy to catch, and the game already records everything you do in the decision log. This is the Scrum Master's mirror, shown at the Retrospective once something is a habit rather than a one-off.

| Pattern | What the game sees | What it says back |
|---|---|---|
| **Super Hero** | every impediment removed by you, blocks included, none left to the Developers | "You cleared six things this Sprint and the Developers cleared none. Which of them needed you?" |
| **Scrum Police** | the rule enforced with no read of the situation: every mid-Sprint pull refused, the Daily Scrum forced when the team wanted five minutes | "You held the line every time. What did it cost the team's judgement?" |
| **Team Boss** | assigning work to Developers | already refused by the seat gate, with the reason: nobody assigns work to a Developer |
| **Chairman** | the Daily Scrum turned into a report to you - held, but the plan never changes after it | "Three Daily Scrums, no change to the plan. What was the event for?" |
| **Absent** | impediments endured, events skipped, nothing removed | "Four impediments, none removed." |

Never a score. The paper's own line is that it does not condemn the misunderstandings; it offers a different perspective. The game names what happened and lets the Retrospective do the work.

## The Scrum Master's View

This answers the open question about whether each seat gets its own screen. Same artifacts, different emphasis - and the Scrum Master's emphasis is **an impediment board beside the Sprint board they do not own**:

- what is blocking, what is an impediment, what has been cleared and by whom;
- the decision log as it accrues, with the costs already recorded there;
- whether each event has happened this Sprint and whether it stayed in its box;
- the team-health mirror, building through the Sprint rather than produced at the end.

The paper argues for exactly this artifact - a board with to do, in progress and done, transparent to everyone, kept as input to the Review and the Retrospective. It is the one screen that reads across both the Product Backlog and the Sprint Backlog without owning either, which is the job.

## What Stays Teaching

Servant leader, coach, teacher, mentor and change agent are conversational. They belong in the coaching registry from `ZOO_ACCOUNTABILITIES_PROPOSAL.md` §2, alongside the deck's exercises:

- slide 102 ("how can we tell if a Scrum Master is effective?") as a card sort against the manager stance's five duties;
- slide 119 (services to the Scrum Team) as a Retrospective reflection;
- the eight stances themselves as the course debrief question, which `ZOO_COURSE.md` already plans;
- the eight misunderstandings as the card sort behind the Scrum Master's own "?".

## Build Order

1. **Blocks and impediments split.** A `kind` on the impediment, four answers instead of two ("leave it to the team", "remove it", "work around it", "escalate it"), each with an honest cost. A day.
2. **The impediment board.** The Scrum Master's view: what is raised, what was done about it, what it cost. Reads state that already exists.
3. **The mirror.** Patterns detected from the log, shown at the Retrospective in the improvements section that already draws from the log.
4. **Stakeholder requests.** One or two a Sprint, not on the last day: say yes, say no, or route it to the Product Owner. The clearest way to teach that the Scrum Master protects focus by routing rather than blocking.
5. **The card sorts.** Content, not code, once the registry exists.

Steps 1 to 3 give the seat a job. Step 4 gives it the one it is best known for.
